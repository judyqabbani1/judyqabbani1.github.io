# How to put your portfolio online (free, ~15 minutes)

## 1. Make a GitHub account
Go to github.com and sign up. Pick your username carefully, because your site address will be
**yourusername.github.io**. For example, `judyalqabbani` gives you `judyalqabbani.github.io`.

## 2. Create the site repository
1. Click the **+** in the top right, then **New repository**.
2. Name it exactly: `yourusername.github.io` (use your real username).
3. Set it to **Public**, then click **Create repository**.

## 3. Upload the files
1. On the new repo page, click **uploading an existing file**.
2. Unzip the portfolio folder on your computer, open it, and drag **everything inside it**
   (index.html, style.css, main.js, resume.pdf, and the `projects` and `img` folders) into the page.
3. Click **Commit changes**.

## 4. Turn on GitHub Pages
1. In the repo, go to **Settings → Pages**.
2. Under "Build and deployment", choose **Deploy from a branch**, branch **main**, folder **/ (root)**, then **Save**.
3. Wait 1–2 minutes and open **https://yourusername.github.io**. Your site is live. 🎉

## 5. (Optional) Get your free .me domain
1. Apply for the **GitHub Student Developer Pack** at education.github.com/pack using your USC email.
2. Once you're approved, claim your free **.me** domain (1 year) through Namecheap (nc.me), e.g. `judyalqabbani.me`.
3. In your repo: **Settings → Pages → Custom domain**, type your domain and click **Save**.
4. In Namecheap, follow GitHub's guide "Managing a custom domain for your GitHub Pages site" to add the DNS records.
5. Tick **Enforce HTTPS** once it's available.

## Updating the site later
- To change text: open the file on GitHub, click the ✏️ pencil icon, edit, then **Commit changes**.
- To add images: open the `img` folder on GitHub, click **Add file → Upload files**.
- To update your resume: upload a new `resume.pdf` with the same name. It replaces the old one.
