Quarto

A complete 2D digital version of the Quarto board game in Java, with drag-and-drop pieces and full win detection. I wrote all of the code.

Overview

Quarto is played on a 4×4 board with 16 unique pieces. Each piece is a combination of four attributes: black or white, small or tall, square or circle, filled or hollow. You win by placing the fourth piece in a row, column or diagonal where all four pieces share at least one attribute.

This was a two-person project. I wrote all the game logic and code, and my partner handled the documentation and presentation.

What I built

  • Three screens: a home menu, the game board, and a How to Play screen that explains the rules.
  • Drag-and-drop pieces: players drag a piece onto the board, the drop position is mapped to the correct cell, and the piece snaps into place.
  • Piece encoding: each of the 16 pieces has a 4-letter code for its attributes, so checking for a shared attribute becomes a simple text check.
  • Board state: every placement updates the board and all the winning lines that cell belongs to (4 rows, 4 columns, 2 diagonals).
  • Win and draw detection: after every move, all 10 lines are checked against all 8 attribute values. The game announces which player won, or a draw once all 16 pieces are placed.
  • Two-player turns tracked by mouse button (left click for Player 1, right click for Player 2), plus custom styling with JavaFX CSS.

Looking back

I built Quarto early in my degree, and it’s one of the projects that taught me the most about structure. The win check works, but it’s a long chain of if-statements repeated for every line and attribute. Today I’d store each piece as four bits (one per attribute) and check a line with a couple of bitwise operations: a few lines of code instead of hundreds, and much easier to extend.

That lesson (design your data first and the logic gets simple) is something I carried straight into Shadow of the Past, where every system is small and separate.
